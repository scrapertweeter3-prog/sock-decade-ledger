Socks are priced per decade, not per pack

The three pack of dress socks costs $9 and the heel thins through inside a year of office rotation. Nobody budgets for it because each purchase is small. Run it to its real length: a dozen pairs in the drawer, a dozen bought each year at $3 a pair, $36 annually, $720 across twenty years, and 240 pairs fed to the trash.

The lifetime guarantee lane prices differently. Six pairs of guaranteed socks at about $22 a pair is a $132 purchase made once. When a pair wears through, the maker replaces it under the warranty instead of selling you a new one, so the second decade of service arrives with no second invoice. Even if every one of the six died and was replaced twice over, the drawer still costs what it cost in year one.

The twenty year spread between the lanes is $588, which is $29.40 every year for the whole period. The quiet number is the body count: 240 dead cheap pairs against a short stack of warranty envelopes. Durability here is not a fabric story, it is an accounting one, and the guarantee converts a recurring expense into a one time purchase.
